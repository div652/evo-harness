## Comparison

All three runs listed the store (two printed about 814K characters of file paths), ran store-wide scans for environmental-indemnity phrases, and built a population of property matters from path words. T0 did this in an agent-defined regex stage for property, land-acquisition and mortgage terms, and T2 derived 42 matters from its saved inventory. Each run then read indemnity agreements, mortgages, leases and closing papers, and named all five qualifying matters.

The runs differ only in the population and the rate. T0 counted 12 matters (8 positive), T1 counted 15 (12, later 10, positive) and T2 counted 10 (5 positive). Each included secured financings whose documents carry mortgages or environmental indemnity agreements. In T0, the review of the population stage said the regex pulled in secured-lending matters and named one. In T1, the write review said the property-centric population could include financing matters, and four check passes only rearranged the strict and closing-record rates. T2's reviews asked whether the denominator was complete, not whether the matters were real-estate deals.

Agent-defined stages (T0's candidate table and population regex, T1's two full-text scans, T2's inventories, candidate rebuild and rescan) map to existing scan, path-search and listing stages.

Label checks: start notes were ignored four times (folder counts under a store-listing label in T0 and T1, and first candidate reads under an in-depth label in T1 and T2). One end note was ignored (T2 kept the in-depth label for its next reads). The others had no later work of that kind or led to another label.
