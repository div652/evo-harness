## Comparison

All three runs listed the store, ran store-wide extraction scans (each timed out before range or background reruns finished) and path searches for credit-agreement words. They then read the openings of 8-15 executed agreements, extracted covenant sections with scripts, and computed yearly shares.

Each run left out in-scope financings that its own searches had surfaced. T0 never opened a health-tech loan, a diagnostics loan or the REIT facility. T1 never opened a packaging company's mezzanine loan or the REIT facility, and labelled three more as bridge or mezzanine loans from scan snippets alone. T2 never opened the REIT facility, and after a review said its conventional-core scope went beyond the request, it kept excluding six in-scope financings and only relabelled the scope as an interpretation. All three excluded the data-center term loan as a project financing, and all three counted acquisition-related, restructuring or court-supervised agreements from other matters. T0 also gave wrong clients for two matters and called one term loan covenant-lite.

Reviews warned about unopened matters at every screening stage (in T0 and T1 they named one of the missed matters) and about snippet-only evidence. They never questioned counting the out-of-population agreements. T2's agent-defined stages (a per-matter inventory, a name-filtered folder walk, a full file inventory and a 222-document covenant extraction) widened its candidate lists, but the set of agreements it read stayed the same.

Label checks: start notes were ignored twice (a document-type label on a request-keyword path search in T1, and a screening label on a store-wide extraction script in T0). End notes were followed three times: T1 and T2 declared folder listing next after a folder-listing note, and T0 declared in-depth reading next after an in-depth note. The other end notes had no later work of that kind or led to a different label.
