## Comparison

All three runs failed (14, 14 and 17 of 29) and in the same way. Each started with a whole-store glob and a matter-folder count, ran path searches that reached all needed matters, and then moved to store-wide text-extraction scans that timed out on the first try. The pool each run read from came from a second scan and its filters: T0 scanned only documents under litigation and agreement paths and then filtered to prevailing-party phrases, which dropped two needed matters; T1 grepped and filtered the scan output for exact phrases, dropping three; T2 kept the partial output of range scans cut off by the 60 s limit, missing three. In each run the review of that scan stage named the cause (phrase filters in T0 and T1, unfinished scans in T2); T0 and T1 moved on, and T2 only checked the highest matter number and row count.

The later errors repeat across runs: a sibling matter of the same client was opened and cited in place of a needed one (T0 and T2 for one client, T1 for another), needed matters whose settlements had each side bear its own fees were written as non-qualifying (T0 one, T1 four, T2 two), and every answer counted five to eight matters outside the set. The write-stage reviews flagged the widened categories every time; the check stages only reconciled counts, fixed paths or spot-checked facts.

Label checks: the store-listing stage's note was ignored in T0 and T1 (a matter-folder count done under the listing label), T1 consolidated scan output under a screening label and screened new matters under an in-depth label, again ignoring the suggested stage.
