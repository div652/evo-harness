## Comparison

All three runs followed the same arc: whole-store listing, matter-folder listing (T0, T2), several rounds of file-name searches for device, patent and injunction words (the reviews flagged substring noise each time), store-wide extraction scans that timed out, then reads of the same handful of patent matters. Every run read the leading patent matter's preliminary-injunction motion marked as a contingency draft on hold.

The runs split at the write stage. T0 (pass) wrote a no-qualifying-matter answer with that matter and its sibling as near misses, then re-read the decisive documents in three check passes. T2 (pass) read the leading matter in depth over three attempts (settlement, scheduling, permanent-injunction analysis) before writing a strict no-confirmed-matter answer. T1 (fail) read the same documents (including a draft permanent-injunction order and trial-strategy memo) and wrote the matter as qualifying on its injunction work. The critic's reviews do not explain the split: the write-stage reviews of all three runs warned of dropped matters (the opposite risk), and T0's write review even called the strict reading a possible narrowing. Only T1's check-stage review named the reliance on draft papers, and T1 finished without changing the answer.

Label checks: the stage protocol's label notes fired at most stage starts; T0 counted folders under a store-listing label and T1 ran a store-wide path sweep under a document-type label and screened candidates under a listing-read label, all ignoring the suggested name.
